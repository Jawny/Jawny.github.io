Support

Answers to common questions. If you need more help, email us.

Setting up

How do I turn Web Streams on?

Web Streams works through its Safari extension, called Streams. Safari needs your permission once:

  1. Open the Settings app and go to Apps > Safari > Extensions > Streams.
  2. Turn on Allow Extension.
  3. Then turn it on for the sites you want (next question).

On iOS 26.2 and later, the Open Safari Settings button in the app opens this screen for you, and the app shows when Streams is on.

If you use Safari profiles, extensions are turned on separately for each profile. In Private Browsing, extensions stay off unless you turn on Allow in Private Browsing on the same Settings screen.

How do I turn it on for a website?
  1. In Safari, open the website with the video.
  2. Tap the page menu button at the left of the address bar, then Streams.
  3. Tap Turn on for and the site's name, for example Turn on for example.com. Allow it when Safari asks. The page reloads, and the TV button appears on its videos.

If the site plays its videos in a player from another website, Streams asks for that one too, in the same step. From then on, the site works without asking.

Can I turn it on for every website at once?

Yes. In the same menu, tap Use on all websites, or in Settings > Apps > Safari > Extensions > Streams, set All Websites to Allow. Safari then says the extension can read and alter webpages you visit. Streams uses that only to find videos and put its button on them, and nothing leaves your phone. We recommend turning it on site by site, which is the default.

How do I turn it off for a site?

On that site, tap the page menu button, then Streams, then Turn off for this site. Or change the site to Deny in Settings > Apps > Safari > Extensions > Streams.

The switches in Settings are greyed out

That usually means Screen Time restrictions are on for this device. Check Settings > Screen Time > Content & Privacy Restrictions, or ask whoever manages the device. A work or school device may also block extensions.

There's no TV button on the video
  • Check that Streams is on for the site (see above).
  • Reload the page. Streams starts with the page, so a page that was already open before you turned it on, or before an update, needs a reload.
  • Very small videos don't get a button.
  • If the video is full screen, leave full screen: Safari hides everything on the page there, the button included.

Sending to the TV

My TV isn't in the list

Your iPhone and your TV need to be on the same Wi-Fi network, and the TV needs to be on. On an Apple TV, check Settings > AirPlay and HomeKit. Open Control Center on your iPhone to see whether AirPlay finds the TV at all. A VPN on the phone can also hide the TV.

The TV plays only the sound

Some video players keep sending only the sound, even after Streams turns AirPlay on. When that happens, the TV button changes to Only sound on TV: play in Streams. Tap it, then Open when Safari asks. The app opens the video in its own player and sends it to the TV, and you can lock your iPhone while it plays.

The button says "Protected video"

The video is copy-protected (DRM), as on most paid subscription services. Web Streams leaves protected videos alone. Use the service's own app on your TV, or its own AirPlay button if it has one.

The button says "Can't send this video"

The site plays the video in a format the TV can't play over AirPlay. There's nothing Web Streams can switch on for it.

The TV shows an error, or nothing happens

The TV gets the video straight from the website. Some websites only send their videos to the browser that opened the page, and refuse the TV. There's no way around that in this version. If you think a site should work, tell us which one and what happened.

Does it work with YouTube?

No. YouTube switches AirPlay off in Safari on purpose and keeps the address of its video out of the page, so there's nothing for Web Streams to send. The YouTube app has its own AirPlay button, and there's a YouTube app for Apple TV. Videos on other sites that do the same can't be sent either.

Can I lock my phone or use other apps while the TV plays?

Yes. That's the point: the TV plays the video itself, not a copy of your screen. If you used Play in Streams, the app keeps the video going on the TV while the phone is locked.

How do I stop playing on the TV?

Tap the TV button on the video (it reads On TV) and choose your iPhone in the AirPlay menu. Or open Control Center, touch and hold the playing video's controls, tap the AirPlay button and choose your iPhone. The TV's remote stops it too.

Does it work with Chromecast, Roku or Fire TV?

No. Web Streams uses AirPlay, so it works with Apple TV and with smart TVs that support AirPlay. Many recent smart TVs do; check your TV's settings for AirPlay.

Can it save or record videos?

No. Web Streams only sends the video that's playing to your TV. It never saves, records or converts anything.

Does it work on iPad or Mac?

Not in this version. Web Streams is made for iPhone.

Your purchase

I got a new iPhone. Do I have to buy Web Streams again?

No. Sign in with the same Apple Account, find Web Streams in the App Store and download it again. Then turn its extension on again on the new iPhone (see above).

How do I get a refund?

Purchases are made through Apple, so Apple handles refunds. Go to reportaproblem.apple.com, sign in, choose Request a refund, and select Web Streams. More help: Request a refund from Apple.
