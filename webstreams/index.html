Web Streams for AirPlay

Put the video you're watching in Safari on your TV. The TV plays the video itself, not a copy of your screen, so you can lock your phone or use other apps. Pay once, nothing collected.

Coming soon to the App Store.

How it works

A TV button on the video

On the sites you turn on, a small TV button appears on each video in Safari.

Pick your TV

Tap it and choose your TV. The TV plays the video straight from the website.

AirPlay, back on

Many websites' video players hide AirPlay on the phone. Web Streams finds the stream behind the player and turns AirPlay back on for that video, only when you tap.

Only the sound?

If the TV gets only the sound, the button offers Play in Streams. The app opens the video in its own player and sends it to the TV.

Turn it on once

Web Streams works through its Safari extension, called Streams. Safari needs your permission once, and then once for each site you want it on. The app walks you through it.

  1. Open the Settings app and go to Apps > Safari > Extensions > Streams. Turn on Allow Extension.
  2. In Safari, open a website with a video. Tap the page menu button at the left of the address bar, tap Streams, then turn it on for the site. Allow it when Safari asks; the page reloads.
  3. Play the video and tap the TV button on it.

On iOS 26.2 and later, a button in the app opens the right screen in Settings for you.

Private by design

  • What you watch stays on your phone. The extension looks at a page only to find its video, and only on the sites you've turned on. Nothing about your browsing is sent anywhere.
  • Nothing collected. No account, no analytics, no ads, no tracking, and no server of our own.
  • The video goes straight to your TV. The TV, or the app's player, gets the video from the website, as Safari does. See the privacy policy for the details.

Good to know before you buy

  • Most videos, not all. Web Streams works with most videos that play in Safari.
  • Protected videos can't be sent. Copy-protected (DRM) video, as on paid subscription services, is left alone. The button says so.
  • Some sites switch AirPlay off on purpose and keep their stream out of the page. Their videos can't be sent; use the site's own app on your TV. More on this.
  • A few sites refuse the TV. The TV fetches the video from the website itself, and some sites only send their videos to the browser.
  • Requirements: an iPhone with iOS 18 or later, and an Apple TV or an AirPlay-compatible smart TV on the same Wi-Fi network. Not for iPad in this version.
